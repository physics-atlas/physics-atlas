## 2. Reading the atlas — no account

When you open the site, nothing about you is stored by us. To deliver the pages, our hosting provider, **Cloudflare**, necessarily processes your IP address and technical details of your browser. Requests to our server are logged for security and troubleshooting for **7 days**, after which the logs are deleted. We do not use them to identify anyone.

**Fonts.** The site's fonts are served from our own site; no third party is contacted when you read it.

**In your browser.** The site remembers some choices on your own device (browser storage): colour theme, palette, reduce motion, the map's arrangement, the legend, the depth you read pages at, the pages' sidebar view, the concepts you've ticked as learned, when you last read each personal map's comments (to mark what's new), and drafts of proposals you're writing (with their pictures). These never leave your device unless you submit a proposal. You can delete them at any time through your browser's settings.

We use **no analytics, no advertising and no tracking**.

## 3. Signing in

Signing in is needed only to propose changes to the atlas, to send feedback, and to create, edit or comment on personal maps. You sign in with your **GitHub** account. We receive from GitHub and keep:

- your **GitHub account number** (it never changes), and
- your **GitHub username**.

We do **not** ask GitHub for your e-mail address and don't store your name or picture.

A cookie keeps you signed in for up to **seven days** (or until you sign out). It's strictly necessary for signing in, so no consent is needed for it (§ 25 (2) TDDDG). During sign-in a second cookie, deleted after ten minutes, protects the sign-in against forgery.

**Legal basis:** providing the service you signed in for (Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR).

## 4. Proposals and feedback (the shared atlas)

The shared atlas lives in a public repository on GitHub. When you **propose** a change or **send feedback**, it is published there as a pull request or an issue, **under your GitHub username**, and is visible to anyone who can see the repository. It stays there as part of the atlas's history. Our server's logs record that your username made it.

**Legal basis:** Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR — this is what proposing and sending feedback are.

## 5. Personal maps

Personal maps are maps a person builds themselves and shares with others they choose.

**What we store, in our database:**

- for each signed-in user: GitHub account number, username, and when they first used personal maps;
- the maps: their content (concepts, relations, pages, references to the atlas), who owns each, and who has been added as an editor, commenter or viewer;
- **comments** and replies, with their author;
- a **history** of changes: who changed what, and when.

**Who sees what:**

- The **members** of a map see its content, each other's usernames and its history; all of them except viewers also see its comments.
- **Anyone with a map's share link** sees its content only — not its members, comments or history — and needs no account. The owner can turn a link off at any time.
- Personal maps are not listed or published anywhere else.

**Please don't put other people's personal information in maps or comments.**

**Legal basis:** Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR — providing personal maps to their owner and members.

## 6. How long we keep it

- **Your account data and your maps:** as long as you use personal maps, until you delete them or your account, or ask us to.
- **When you delete your account:** your username and account number are deleted. Maps you own are deleted, with everything in them. In maps owned by others, your comments and changes stay, but are shown as by **"former member"** — they are part of the shared work. If you'd like your comments removed too, tell us and we'll remove them.
- **Proposals and feedback on GitHub:** they remain in the public repository as part of the atlas's history; GitHub's own terms apply there.
- **Request logs:** 7 days (section 2). **Database backups:** deleted data can remain in the database's restore points for up to **30 days**.

## 9. Age

Reading the atlas and opening shared maps needs no account. Signing in is for people aged **16 or over**.

## 10. Security

Connections to the site are encrypted. The database is encrypted where it's stored, and only reachable through our server, which checks on every request whether you may see or change a map. Share links are long random codes. Access to the hosting and repository accounts is protected with two-factor sign-in.
